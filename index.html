The Advertising Problem, Stated Plainly
You cannot advertise this site profitably today — and that is fixable in a week

There is no GA4 tag, no tag manager and no conversion tracking in your served page source. That is not a small technical nicety. It is the difference between advertising and guessing.

Without tracking, here is what you can know you spent the money Here is what you cannot know: Which search brought the enquiry ✗ Which page they were on when they called ✗ What a kitchen enquiry actually costs you ✗ Which of your services pays and which loses ✗

Google's own bidding needs conversion data to optimise against. With none, it optimises for clicks — and clicks are not jobs. This is the first thing we would fix, before a penny of ad spend, and it is the cheapest item on the whole list.

The Title Tag — Small Item, Large Consequence
Your homepage title is the blue line a searcher reads in Google
What yours says now Dial Carpentry What it could say Carpenter & Kitchen Fitter in Swansea & Gower | Dial Carpentry Meta description ✗ none written — Google invents one

Somebody searching “kitchen fitter Swansea” sees a list of blue links. Yours carries no trade word and no town, so it matches nothing they typed and reads as the least relevant result on the page — regardless of the fact that you are the most experienced carpenter on it.

Your inner pages are named correctly. That is what makes this frustrating rather than structural: the hard part is already done and the front door is the bit that was missed.

Twenty Years Of Work And One Testimonial
This is the largest single gap on the site

You have been trading since 2006. There is one named testimonial on the website — M Hartnell, Gower — and no star rating or review count anywhere.

A homeowner choosing who fits a £12,000 kitchen is comparing three carpenters in a browser tab each. Two of them will show a number. The number is doing the persuading, and you are not in that comparison — not because the work is worse, but because twenty years of satisfied customers were never asked to write anything down.

Nothing on this whole page is cheaper to fix. It costs a message to your last ten customers.

What A Homeowner Asks Before Booking — And What Your Site Answers

"Are they insured?" ✗ not mentioned anywhere "Are they qualified?" ✗ no NVQ, FMB, TrustMark shown "Do other people rate them?" ✗ one testimonial, no count "Is the work any good?" ✓ yes — your photos answer this well

You answer the one question that is hardest to fake and none of the three that are easy. If you hold public liability cover and any qualification, publishing them takes an afternoon and moves four of the red rows above.

We have not assumed you hold any of these. If you do not, say so on the call and we will take them off the list rather than build a page around them.

Fix First — No Spend Required
  • Rewrite the homepage title tag and add a meta description. Ten minutes. It is your Google headline and it currently says nothing
  • Install GA4 and conversion tracking. Nothing can be advertised or improved until calls and enquiries are counted
  • Ask your last ten customers for a Google review. Twenty years of goodwill, one testimonial published
  • Publish your insurance and any qualifications — if you hold them, they answer three of the four questions above
Build Next
  • An enquiry form on the homepage itself — asking the job type, rough budget and timescale, so you can price the good ones first
  • A star rating and review count shown on every page, not one paragraph on one page
  • A landlord and rental page — repeat commercial work, and you already do it
  • Then advertising, pointed at kitchen and renovation searches in Swansea and the Gower, measured from day one

Twenty years of work, three good service pages, and nothing counting the phone calls.

The craft side of this is already right. What is missing is the measurement and the proof — and those are the two cheapest things on the list.
